Research

From molecular mechanisms to material properties

I investigate how polymer architecture, thermodynamics and processing history determine the structure, mechanics and degradation of soft and polymeric materials.

1 / 5
01

Semicrystalline polymers: topology, morphology and interfaces

Semicrystalline polymers feature a combination of ordered crystalline domains and amorphous domains. I study how processing determines the structural features of the semicrystalline nanostructure as well as the topology of the amorphous domains. In recent work, I described how melt-memory effects impact crystallinity and chain orientatio near the interface of welded plastics parts.

Chain topologyCrystallizationThermal weldingMolecular dynamics
02

Polymer degradation and micro-nanoplastics

I investigate the role of polymer microstructure—particularly amorphous topology and the organization of semicrystalline domains—in micro- and nanoplastics formation and release. I connect degradation mechanisms to changes in microstructure and mechanics to help develop predictive models for lifetime part performance.

NanoplasticsPhotooxidationAmorphous domainsPolymer aging
03

Thermodynamics and fluid sorption in polymers

In my doctoral research, I developed molecular thermodynamic descriptions of fluid sorption in semicrystalline and amorphous polymers. I modeled water–polyethylene glycol mixtures with SAFT-γ Mie, hydrocarbon sorption in polyethylene, and CO₂ solubility in glassy polystyrene and PMMA.

SAFT-γ MieFluid sorptionEquations of stateStatistical thermodynamics
04

Processing, mechanics and multiphysics interfaces

I develop and apply modeling frameworks for thermoplastic processing that couple thermal transport, crystallization, deformation and molecular-scale dynamics. I focus on how interfaces form in overmolded thermoplastic parts, and integrate these insight in continuum models for predicting part-level mechanical behavior.

OvermoldingChemo-thermo-mechanicsInterfacial integrityConstitutive modeling
05

Polymer networks, gels and programmable matter

I study the thermodynamics of gels and vitrimers, focusing on the effect of crosslink density and boundary conditions on equilibrium properties and phase behavior. I also study DNA self-assembly, aiming to develop predictive models of the assembly of DNA origami superlattices.

Polymer networksGel thermodynamicsReversible crosslinksDNA self-assembly
06

Methods: theory, computation and experiment

I combine physics-based modeling, simulation and experimental characterization. I employ statistical mechanics, polymer physics and self-consistent field theory; molecular dynamics, Monte Carlo and density functional theory; scientific programming and HPC; and SAXS/WAXS, calorimetry, thermogravimetric analysis, GC–MS, FTIR and Raman spectroscopy.
